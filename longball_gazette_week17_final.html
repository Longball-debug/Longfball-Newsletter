Week 17 Final — July 27–August 2, 2026
RATS TAKE DOWN GAMBLERS; CASCADES ROLL ON; A THREE-WAY KNOT FORMS FOR THE LAST PLAYOFF SPOT
Two weeks remain in the 2026 MLBLONGBALL regular season, and the top of the standings is starting to separate from the bottom — but the fight for the final playoff berth just got a lot more interesting. Three teams sit tied at 8-9 with the clock running out.
By the Longball Gazette Wire Staff
League Headquarters — Sunday, August 2, 2026

The Desert Rats did what they needed to do. A 475.33-to-402.67 win over the Houston Gamblers moves the commissioner's squad to 12-5, keeps them two games back of the league-leading Troutdale Cascades, and pushes the Gamblers to 7-10 with two weeks left to save a fading season. Nico Hoerner (44.5 pts), Alex Bregman (41.5 pts), and Shohei Ohtani (40.5 pts) combined for three of the top eight hitting performances of the week, and the Rats needed every bit of it — Ceddanne Rafaela put up 53.5 points on the other side, the single best individual performance in MLBLONGBALL this week, and it still wasn't enough to save Houston.

The Troutdale Cascades, meanwhile, kept doing what the Cascades have done all season: winning. A 427.00-to-375.50 handling of Sidd Finch keeps the Cascades at an untouchable 14-3 and the clear favorite to close out the Blue Division and the No. 1 overall seed. Sidd Finch drops to 6-11, extends a season-worst six-game losing streak, and is now effectively out of the playoff conversation with two weeks to play.

But the real story of Week 17 is buried in the standings, not the scoreboard: with the Cascades, Rats, Newberry Indians, and Goldy Goldy Gone pulling away at the top and Tempe Scorpions locking down the White Division despite a losing record, the sixth and final playoff spot has come down to a dead-even three-way tie at 8-9 between the Midland Red Weasel Dogs, The BANK of Texas, and the Troutdale Gales. Two weeks remain to sort it out.

Indians Win Sixth Straight; Dogs Fall Further Behind

The Newberry Indians throttled the Midland Red Weasel Dogs 441.00 to 364.17, extending Newberry's win streak to six games and locking in their spot as Red Division champions with two weeks to spare. Francisco Lindor (43 pts) and Walbert Urena (47 pts) both cracked the week's top performers. The Dogs fall to 8-9 and now find themselves in a dogfight — pun intended — for the final wildcard spot.

BANK Survives Goldy By 6.17; Gales Handle Sons of Pitches

The BANK of Texas edged Goldy Goldy Gone 374.00 to 367.83 in the tightest game of the week, extending BANK's win streak to three and dragging them into the three-way tie atop the wildcard chase. Troutdale Gales, for their part, took care of business against the Sons of Pitches, 350.00 to 298.17, their second straight win and their own entry into that same 8-9 knot. Goldy falls to 10-7 but remains safely inside the playoff field for now.

Six Spots, One Knot, and Not Much Time
By Joe Falls  ·  J.G. Taylor Spink Award Winner, 2001  ·  The Longball Gazette

Two weeks. That is what is left of the MLBLONGBALL regular season, and as of Sunday night, three teams are tied at 8-9 for the sixth and final playoff spot. The Midland Red Weasel Dogs. The BANK of Texas. The Troutdale Gales. Same record. Same number of games left. Different paths to get here, and no guarantee any of them finishes on top.

I have covered enough of these fights to know they rarely resolve cleanly. The BANK is riding a three-game win streak and made three separate waiver moves this week alone, cutting Royce Lewis and Freddy Peralta loose in the process — that is not a team playing it safe. The Gales just beat the Sons of Pitches by 51 points for their second straight win. And Midland, who made three roster moves of their own trying to find an edge, just got run over by Newberry 441 to 364. All three are moving, but only one of them moves on.

Meanwhile, up top, nothing has changed and that is the story in itself. The Cascades are 14-3. The Rats are 12-5. Two games separate them and have for weeks. Newberry Indians have now won six in a row and locked the Red Division. Tempe Scorpions somehow lead the White Division at 7-10 — the only division winner in the league with a losing record, which tells you everything about how competitive that division has been all year.

Let's talk about Sandy Alcantara for a second, because 55.5 points on 13 innings with 10 strikeouts and two wins is the best pitching week in this league all season, and Sidd Finch still lost by 51 points to the Cascades. That is not a knock on Alcantara. That is a measure of how good Troutdale has been. When your ace throws a complete two-start week like that and it still isn't enough, you are not playing a fair fight. Sidd Finch is 6-11 now, losers of six straight, and their season is effectively about pride at this point.

Ceddanne Rafaela put up 53.5 points for the Houston Gamblers — four homers, ten RBI, the best hitting week in the league — and Houston still lost by 72 points to the Desert Rats. That's twice in one week we saw an individually dominant performance get buried by a team effort on the other side. This league is deep enough now that one great week from one player isn't close to enough. You need three or four good weeks from three or four players, which is exactly what the Rats got from Hoerner, Bregman, and Ohtani.

What Six Straight Wins Actually Means
By Mitch Albom  ·  Detroit Free Press  ·  The Longball Gazette

There is a version of this season where the Newberry Indians never get talked about the way they deserve. They are not the team with the gaudy overall record — that belongs to Troutdale. They are not the team drawing headlines for a two-point thriller or a season-low collapse. They are just the team that keeps winning, quietly, six weeks in a row now, and has clinched a division while half the league was distracted by everyone else's drama.

Francisco Lindor gave them 43 points this week. Walbert Urena gave them 47. Neither was the best performance in the league at their position, and neither needed to be. The Indians do not need a signature week from any one player because they have found a way to get contributions from everywhere, every week, for a month and a half. That is not luck. Luck does not survive six consecutive scoring periods. That is a team that figured something out.

I want to say something about Ithaca Spartans and Sons of Pitches, because 4-13 and 6-11 are hard numbers to sit with, and there are real people behind those rosters who have been showing up every week since March hoping for a different outcome. Gerrit Cole gave the Spartans 47.83 points this week — a genuinely excellent start — in a game his team still lost. Yordan Alvarez gave the Sons 51.5 points, the second-best hitting week in the league, in a game his team still lost by 51. That is the cruelty of this format. Individual brilliance does not save a season that has already gone sideways.

And yet there is something worth watching in both of those rosters over these final two weeks — not for the standings, which are mostly settled for them, but for what comes next. A team that gets 51 points from Yordan Alvarez in a lost season is a team with a foundation. What Ithaca and the Sons do with these last two weeks says more about 2027 than it does about 2026.

Big Lou’s Corner: The Rats Are Fine, But I'm Still Watching the Commissioner
By Lou Palmieri, Baseball Lifer  ·  The Longball Gazette

Let's get the obvious thing out of the way: the Desert Rats won by 72 points this week, they're 12-5, and they're two games back of the best team in the league. That's a good week. I have questions anyway, because I always have questions when the commissioner's team is involved, and this week the commissioner's team went out and grabbed Luis Rengifo off waivers while everyone else was distracted by the Newberry Indians winning their sixth straight. Convenient timing. I'm not saying anything. I'm just saying I noticed.

Now, Hoerner, Bregman, and Ohtani combined for 126.5 points, and that's a legitimately great week from a legitimately great roster, and I'll give credit where it's due. But I want the record to show that the guy running the league picked up a middle infielder with two weeks left in a season where he's chasing first place. If the Rats catch the Cascades in the next two weeks, I am formally opening an investigation. A friendly one. But an investigation.

THE BANK OF TEXAS MADE THREE MOVES IN ONE WEEK. Three! Kyle Stowers in, Royce Lewis out. Pete Fairbanks in, Samy Natera Jr. out. Merrill Kelly in, Freddy Peralta out. This is a franchise that has spent this entire season being characterized, fairly, as a team that panics and drops guys early and reaches for relievers. And you know what? It's working right now. Three-game win streak. Tied for the last playoff spot. Sometimes the crazy thing is the right thing. I don't know how to feel about that and I've been doing this a long time.

SANDY ALCANTARA THREW THIRTEEN INNINGS AND STRUCK OUT TEN AND HIS TEAM STILL LOST BY FIFTY-ONE POINTS. I need everyone to understand how absurd that sentence is. That's a WEEK from a starting pitcher, not a start. Fifty-five and a half points. Best pitching line in the league. And Sidd Finch is losers of six straight anyway because the Cascades are the Cascades and nobody's stopping them right now, not even a two-win week from an ace.
