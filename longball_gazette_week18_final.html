Week 18 Final — One Week to Go
Sidd Finch Ends the Streak, Four Playoff Spots Are Locked — and Two Are Still Very Much Alive
Four teams are safely through to the 2026 playoffs. The final two spots — and the White Division title — will be decided in the Week 19 finale. Here is everything that happened in Scoring Period 18, and everything still on the line in the last week of the MLBLONGBALL regular season.
By Duke Falcone
MLBLONGBALL LEAGUE OFFICE — August 9, 2026

Newberry Indians had won six straight. They had clinched the Red Division. They were rolling into the season's final stretch looking like the hottest team nobody was talking about. Then Sidd Finch happened. A 455.33-to-331.17 beatdown that had nothing to do with a slump and everything to do with a team that decided this was the week to remind the league it still had teeth. The streak is over. The Indians' spot in the field is not in question — but the loss is a reminder that nothing is guaranteed once October baseball starts.

Four teams are safely in regardless of what happens in Week 19: the Troutdale Cascades (Blue Division champs, best record in the league), the Desert Rats (Wild Card, fresh off the week's high score of 559.50), the Newberry Indians (Red Division champs), and Goldy Goldy Gone (Wild Card). The last two spots are genuinely up for grabs. Midland Red Weasel Dogs and Troutdale Gales are knotted at 9-9, with the Weasel Dogs holding the tiebreaker — the Gales need a win over Goldy Goldy Gone and a Weasel Dogs loss to Tempe Scorpions to leap them. And in the White Division, Tempe Scorpions and Houston Gamblers are tied at 7-11, with the Scorpions holding the tiebreaker — the Gamblers need a win over the Cascades and a Scorpions loss to Midland to steal the division and the final playoff seed.

Same story in White: Houston Gamblers (7-11) need a win over the Cascades and a Scorpions loss to steal the division.

Four In, Two Up for Grabs, and the Gales Aren't Done Yet
By Duke Falcone  ·  The Longball Gazette

Troutdale Gales won their game. Jesus Luzardo threw fifteen innings, walked away with a 1.20 ERA and nineteen strikeouts, and put up the single best pitching week anybody in this league produced all season. The Gales beat Houston by nearly thirty points. And on Monday morning they're still on the outside of the playoff field — tied at 9-9 with Midland Red Weasel Dogs, losing the tiebreaker. That's fantasy baseball in a twelve-team league with a tiebreaker rule nobody thinks about until it's the only thing standing between them and October.

Here's the thing, though: it isn't over. Beat Goldy Goldy Gone this week, get a Weasel Dogs loss to Tempe Scorpions, and the Gales are in. Luzardo just turned in the best pitching week anyone in this league has produced all season in exactly the week they needed it. Do it again, and get a little help, and Troutdale's got a real shot. I'm not calling it likely. I'm calling it alive, which is more than a lot of teams can say with one week left.

Meanwhile the Tempe Scorpions are currently holding a playoff spot at 7-and-11. Say that out loud. A losing record, a shaky grip on a division title, and it's not even locked up yet. I don't make the rules and honestly I don't love the rules, but credit where it's due: the White Division has been a disaster all year and Tempe's the one hanging on. Houston Gamblers, also 7-11, get one more shot to steal it — but they have to beat the best team in the league to do it. That's a tall order.

Sidd Finch snapping Newberry's six-game streak is the story nobody saw coming. The Indians had the division locked up and still lost by 124 points to a team playing out the string. If I'm Newberry, I'm not panicking — the division title's banked — but I'm also not forgetting that score before the playoffs start.

What a Tiebreaker Takes From You
By Marty Aldrich  ·  The Longball Gazette

There is a version of next Sunday where Troutdale Gales are a playoff team, and it runs through exactly one week of baseball. Nine wins, nine losses, a three-game winning streak arriving right on time. Somewhere in a spreadsheet, a tiebreaker currently says Midland Red Weasel Dogs instead of the Gales, and the spreadsheet does not care that Jesus Luzardo threw the best week any pitcher threw all year to get Troutdale here. But the spreadsheet is not done talking. One more week remains, and this time the Gales control it themselves.

I keep coming back to what it means to arrive at the final week still needing something from a team you can't control. The Gales beat Houston on their own; now they need Goldy Goldy Gone at home and a Weasel Dogs loss to Tempe Scorpions on someone else's field. Their offense ranked among the league's best much of this season. Their pitching, for one week, was the best anyone produced. Whether that's enough by Sunday night depends on a game they're not even playing in.

Contrast that with Tempe Scorpions, seven wins and eleven losses, holding a division title heading into the finale because the three teams around them fell further. There is something almost generous about a system that lets you outlast collapse rather than achieve greatness — right up until the moment it demands you finish the job yourself. The Scorpions have not had a good season. They will get one more week to have a good week, and Houston Gamblers, tied with them at 7-11, get one more week to take it away.

And Newberry — twelve wins, a division locked up for weeks, a six-game streak that felt inevitable until Sidd Finch decided it wasn't. Sometimes a loss with nothing at stake tells you more than a win with everything on the line. The Indians will want to answer that before the games start counting for real. I suspect they will. I suspect it will not be enough to erase what Sunday looked like.

Tout Wars Corner: How the Experts Think About Week 19-Type Games
H2H Points Strategy, Aggregated for MLBLONGBALL  ·  The Longball Gazette

The consensus among the fantasy industry's top head-to-head points minds — the crowd that competes annually in Tout Wars' H2H format — is that the final week of a regular season, particularly one where playoff seeding is still moving, is exactly the wrong time to bench a struggling regular. Small-sample overreactions to one bad week are how good rosters lose meaningful seeding games; the analysts who consistently cash in these formats tend to trust full-season projections over a single ugly line.

The other recurring theme in published H2H strategy: stream aggressively in the final week if a matchup edge is truly marginal, because there is no next week to recover innings or at-bats you left on the bench. That applies directly to Troutdale Gales and Houston Gamblers this week — both still alive for a playoff seed and both needing every point they can find. For the four MLBLONGBALL teams with nothing left to play for but consolation-bracket seeding, the logic flips: there's an argument for resting rostered stars and auditioning wire pieces who might matter for keeper or draft-strategy purposes next spring. Either way, the through-line from the experts is the same: know which kind of week you're playing before you set your lineup.
