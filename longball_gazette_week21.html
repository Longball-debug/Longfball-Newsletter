Cascades, Desert Rats Set for Two-Week War as Championship Field Narrows to Two
Top-seeded Troutdale outlasts Goldy Goldy Gone; No. 2 Desert Rats survive a scare from the Scorpions to book the Final
Longball Gazette Staff · Week 21 Semi-Final Recap

The chase for the Season 19 crown is down to two teams, and it took everything both semifinalists had to get there. The top-seeded Troutdale Cascades needed a 419.33-point Round 2 explosion to hold off Goldy Goldy Gone's late offensive push, 419.33–400, while the No. 2 seed Desert Rats survived a week-long dogfight with the White Division champion Tempe Scorpions, escaping 397.33–332.17 after Tempe kept the margin inside 15 points for most of the week.

The Final Round now stretches across two weeks — Aug 31 through Sep 13 — with the Desert Rats looking to avenge last year's finish and the Cascades chasing a title behind a pitching staff that has carried them through both playoff rounds.

On the other side of the bracket, the race for the No. 1 overall pick in next year's draft is set: the Troutdale Gales, who blew out Sons of Pitches 370.33–254.17, will face The BANK of Texas, fresh off a 365.67–306 win over Sidd Finch. Unlike the Championship, that field will settle in a single week — the winner of this one-week Final claims the top selection.

Elsewhere around the league, the waiver wire stayed hot all the way through Sunday's deadline, with Kyle Tucker and Bo Bichette both changing hands out of the Desert Rats' clubhouse and into Troutdale Cascades'. Full story inside.

Tucker and Bichette Both Change Zip Codes — Straight Out of Arizona

The week's headline waiver story played out entirely between two clubs. The Desert Rats, needing catcher and infield help ahead of their semi-final, cut loose both Kyle Tucker and Bo Bichette in separate moves on Thursday, Aug 27 — and Troutdale Cascades, already through to the Final, scooped up both.

Desert Rats Rebuild the Bullpen on the Fly

With their semi-final against Tempe still in the balance, the Desert Rats ran through six separate priority-waiver bullpen moves during the week, cycling through arms almost daily: Craig Kimbrel out for Adrian Morejon, Morejon out for Nate Pearson, Garrett Cleavinger in and out twice, Jordan Romano in, Elvis Alvarado in and out, and finally Orion Kerkering claimed twice in the same 24-hour window. The RP streaming approach paid off — Morejon (21.5) and Alvarez (23.5) both delivered in the win over Tempe before being flipped for the next wave of matchups.

Goldy Goldy Gone Stayed Busiest of All — Even in Defeat

No team touched the wire more than Goldy Goldy Gone, which made more than 30 separate claim/drop moves during the week despite ultimately falling to Troutdale Cascades in the semis. The club cycled through catchers (Ruiz, Murphy, Herrera), relievers (Cleavinger, Minter, Gaddis, Pagan) and multi-position bats (Sogard, Tim Tawa, Edmundo Sosa) almost daily, seemingly auditioning next season's roster in real time even as the current one was being eliminated.

Joe Falls

Longball Gazette Columnist

A Championship Final Worthy of the Name

There is a particular flavor of fantasy baseball drama that only shows up once a year, and we got a full dose of it this week. Two rounds down, one team's whole season riding on the next two, and the two survivors of the Championship bracket are exactly who you'd want standing at the end — a pitching-rich Troutdale Cascades club and a lineup-deep Desert Rats squad that has now beaten the same team twice this month, first in the standings and then in the bracket.

I keep coming back to that Logan Webb non-start for Arizona. In a matchup decided by 65 points, a 16-point unforced error like that should have been the difference. It wasn't, and that tells you something about the gap between these two rosters and the rest of the field. The Rats can survive their own mistakes right now. Not every team left standing after Week 12 could say the same.

As for Troutdale, Parker Messick's 48-point week is the kind of individual performance that swings a two-week Final before it even starts. If he gives them even half of that in Week 22, Goldy Goldy Gone's late charge will look like a footnote instead of a warning sign.

MA

Mitch Albom

Longball Gazette Columnist

The Ones Who Almost Made It

Every playoff round has a team that goes down swinging, and this week it was Goldy Goldy Gone. Down the stretch of a semi-final they'd eventually lose, Goldy's front office didn't stop working — more than thirty waiver moves in a single week, most of them made after the outcome was functionally decided. There's something almost admirable in that. You don't have to like the results to respect the refusal to just let the week play out.

And credit where it's due to Jac Caglianone and Otto Lopez, who combined for 64.5 points and gave Goldy a hitting lead deep into the week before Troutdale's pitching staff simply buried them. Sons of Pitches and Sidd Finch went out quieter, but Jeremy Peña's three-homer week and Pete Crow-Armstrong's four-homer, seven-RBI eruption deserve to be remembered even in a loss. Sometimes the box score from an elimination game is the best one a player puts up all year.

The bracket doesn't care about any of that now. But somebody should.

BL

Big Lou Palmieri

Longball Gazette Columnist

Somebody Tell the Cascades Kyle Tucker Isn't a Rental Car

I need somebody in Troutdale's front office to explain this to me slowly. You win a $255 FAAB bid on Kyle Tucker — a legitimate, quality outfielder — on Saturday morning. You already clinched your spot in the Championship Final. And by Sunday afternoon, you've flipped him for Chase DeLauter. Thirty-six hours. That's not a roster move, that's a layover.

Meanwhile, over in the Draft-Position bracket, Sidd Finch dropped $998 in fake money to win Robbie Ray for seventeen real dollars, in a week where the season was already circling the drain. Ray went out and put up 24.67 points in a loss. Somewhere, a version of this league exists where that bid gets made in Week 3 instead of Week 21, and Sidd is playing for something other than pride right now.

Say this for the Desert Rats, though — while everyone else was making headlines, they were quietly running six bullpen moves through the priority wire and getting 45 combined points out of Morejon and Alvarez in a semi-final win. No fireworks. Just production. That's usually how you end up in the last two teams standing.
