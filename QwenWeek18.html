Matchup Recaps

Desert Rats @ Houston Gamblers

Rats win by 72.66 · Rats 12-5 · Gamblers 7-10

Ceddanne Rafaela did everything a fantasy owner could ask for a losing team: 53.5 points, four home runs, ten RBI — the single best hitting performance in MLBLONGBALL this week. It was not enough. The Desert Rats answered with balance instead of a single monster line — Nico Hoerner (44.5), Alex Bregman (41.5), and Shohei Ohtani (40.5) combined for 126.5 points on their own, and that depth is why the Rats sit 12-5 and two games back of first.

Houston falls to 7-10 and out of the top of the White Division picture. A team that gets 53 points from one player and still loses by 72 has bigger issues than one bad matchup.

Rats depth: Hoerner 44.5 · Bregman 41.5 · Ohtani 40.5Rafaela 53.5 (4 HR, 10 RBI)Margin 72.66

Goldy Goldy Gone @ BANK of Texas

BANK wins by 6.17 · BANK 8-9 · Goldy 10-7

The closest game of Week 17 went to The BANK of Texas by a margin thinner than most single stat lines. Cristopher Sanchez chipped in 35 points on the mound, part of a staff that has now won three straight weeks after a season defined more by panic drops than patience. This week, the moves held.

Goldy falls to 10-7 but remains safely inside the playoff field. A six-point margin stings more than a blowout — and it is exactly the kind of game that could matter when the season's final tiebreakers get sorted.

Tempe Scorpions @ Ithaca Spartans

Scorpions win by 22.50 · Scorpions 7-10 · Spartans 4-13

Gavin Williams put together the week's second-best pitching line — 54.33 points on 12.2 innings and 22 strikeouts — enough to hold off Gerrit Cole (47.83), who simply didn't have the supporting cast to keep pace. Tempe improves to 7-10 and, remarkably, stays atop the White Division — a division that has yet to produce a team playing .500 baseball.

Ithaca falls to 4-13, the worst record in the league; their playoff hopes were mathematically difficult before this week and are now essentially theoretical.

Midland Red Weasel Dogs @ Newberry Indians

Indians win by 76.83 · Indians 12-5 · Dogs 8-9

Six in a row for the Newberry Indians, quietly one of the league's most dangerous second-half teams. Francisco Lindor (43) and Walbert Urena (47) both landed in the week's top performers, and 441.00 points were comfortably the second-highest total of the week. Newberry clinches the Red Division with two weeks to spare.

Midland falls to 8-9, snapping any waiver-wire momentum, and now finds themselves in a flat three-way tie for the final playoff spot. The Dogs churned through four roster moves in the past week; Week 17 wasn't the week it showed up.

Troutdale Gales @ Sons of Pitches

Gales win by 51.83 · Gales 8-9 · Sons 6-11

Yordan Alvarez put up 51.5 points — the week's second-best individual hitting performance — and it wasn't nearly enough. The Gales won going away for their second straight victory, climbing into the three-way tie for the final playoff spot at 8-9. This is a team that looked lost as recently as a month ago.

The Sons fall to 6-11, effectively ending any realistic playoff math. Alvarez's monster week is a bright spot on an otherwise deflating season.

Sidd Finch @ Troutdale Cascades

Cascades win by 51.50 · Cascades 14-3 · Finch 6-11

Sandy Alcantara delivered the single best pitching performance of the week — 55.5 points on 13 innings, 10 strikeouts, two wins — and Sidd Finch still lost by more than 50. That is what facing the Cascades looks like right now. Chase Burns (39) kept the pitching humming, and Kade Anderson's waiver addition continues a Seattle-Mariner pattern this league has noticed for years.

The Cascades move to 14-3 and have all but locked up the Blue Division and the No. 1 seed. Sidd Finch falls to 6-11 on a season-worst six-game losing streak.

JF
Joe FallsJ.G. Taylor Spink Award Winner, 2001

Six Spots, One Knot, and Not Much Time

Two weeks. That is what is left of the MLBLONGBALL regular season, and as of Sunday night, three teams are tied at 8-9 for the sixth and final playoff spot. The Midland Red Weasel Dogs. The BANK of Texas. The Troutdale Gales. Same record. Same number of games left. Different paths to get here, and no guarantee any of them finishes on top.

I have covered enough of these fights to know they rarely resolve cleanly. The BANK is riding a three-game win streak and made three waiver moves this week alone, cutting Royce Lewis and Freddy Peralta loose — that is not a team playing it safe. The Gales just beat the Sons by 51 points for their second straight win. And Midland, who made three roster moves of their own, just got run over by Newberry 441 to 364. All three are moving, but only one of them moves on.

Meanwhile, up top, nothing has changed and that is the story in itself. The Cascades are 14-3. The Rats are 12-5. Newberry has won six in a row and locked the Red Division. Tempe somehow leads the White Division at 7-10 — the only division winner with a losing record, which tells you everything about that division.

Let's talk about Sandy Alcantara: 55.5 points on 13 innings with 10 strikeouts and two wins is the best pitching week in this league all season, and Sidd Finch still lost by 51. That is not a knock on Alcantara. That is a measure of how good Troutdale has been. When your ace throws a complete two-start week and it still isn't enough, you are not playing a fair fight.

Ceddanne Rafaela put up 53.5 for Houston — four homers, ten RBI — and Houston still lost by 72. Twice in one week we saw an individually dominant performance get buried by a team effort. You need three or four good weeks from three or four players — which is exactly what the Rats got from Hoerner, Bregman, and Ohtani.

MA
Mitch AlbomDetroit Free Press

What Six Straight Wins Actually Means

There is a version of this season where the Newberry Indians never get talked about the way they deserve. They are not the team with the gaudy overall record — that belongs to Troutdale. They are just the team that keeps winning, quietly, six weeks in a row now, and has clinched a division while half the league was distracted by everyone else's drama.

Francisco Lindor gave them 43 points this week. Walbert Urena gave them 47. Neither was the best performance in the league at their position, and neither needed to be. The Indians do not need a signature week from any one player because they get contributions from everywhere, every week, for a month and a half. That is not luck. Luck does not survive six consecutive scoring periods. That is a team that figured something out.

I want to say something about Ithaca and the Sons of Pitches, because 4-13 and 6-11 are hard numbers to sit with. Gerrit Cole gave the Spartans 47.83 points — a genuinely excellent start — in a game his team still lost. Yordan Alvarez gave the Sons 51.5, the second-best hitting week in the league, in a game his team still lost by 51. That is the cruelty of this format. Individual brilliance does not save a season that has already gone sideways.

And yet there is something worth watching in both rosters over these final two weeks — not for the standings, but for what comes next. A team that gets 51 points from Yordan Alvarez in a lost season is a team with a foundation. What Ithaca and the Sons do with these last two weeks says more about 2027 than it does about 2026.

LP
Big Lou PalmieriBaseball Lifer

The Rats Are Fine, But I'm Still Watching the Commissioner

Let's get the obvious out of the way: the Desert Rats won by 72 points, they're 12-5, and they're two games back of the best team in the league. That's a good week. I have questions anyway, because I always have questions when the commissioner's team is involved — and this week the commissioner's team grabbed Luis Rengifo off waivers while everyone else was distracted by Newberry's sixth straight. Convenient timing. I'm not saying anything. I'm just saying I noticed.

Hoerner, Bregman, and Ohtani combined for 126.5 points — a legitimately great week from a legitimately great roster, and I'll give credit where it's due. But the record will show that the guy running the league picked up a middle infielder with two weeks left while chasing first place. If the Rats catch the Cascades, I am formally opening an investigation. A friendly one. But an investigation.

THE BANK OF TEXAS MADE THREE MOVES IN ONE WEEK. Three! This is a franchise characterized, fairly, as a team that panics and drops guys early. And you know what? It's working. Three-game win streak. Tied for the last playoff spot. Sometimes the crazy thing is the right thing. I don't know how to feel about that.

SANDY ALCANTARA THREW THIRTEEN INNINGS AND STRUCK OUT TEN AND HIS TEAM STILL LOST BY FIFTY-ONE POINTS. I need everyone to understand how absurd that sentence is. That's a WEEK from a starting pitcher. Best pitching line in the league — and Sidd Finch is losers of six straight anyway, because the Cascades are the Cascades and nobody's stopping them right now.
