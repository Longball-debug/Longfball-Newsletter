Above the Fold

Cascades Selling Playoff Tickets in Troutdale — Spartans Selling the Whole Franchise

By the Gazette Staff · Week 14 · Period 14 Final

Troutdale's Cascades handled Ithaca 421 to 361.33 in Week 14 and haven't lost since the calendar flipped to June, pushing their record to a league-best 11-3. The margin wasn't just on the scoreboard — it was in the mood. One clubhouse is measuring up for a parade route. The other is reportedly fielding calls about a sale.

The Ithaca Spartans, mathematically buried at 4-10 and dead last in the Combined standings, saw their loss to Troutdale accompanied by tongue-in-cheek reports that ownership is exploring "strategic alternatives" for the franchise ahead of 2027. Ithaca's pitching staff has been the primary culprit all year, sitting last in the league in pitching FPts (1,363.5) by a wide margin.

Meanwhile, second-place Desert Rats (10-4) kept pace with a 493.33-435.67 win over Midland, exploiting what can only be described as a pitching no-show from the Weasel Dogs early in the week. Newberry (9-5) is quietly the league's hottest team — a wire-to-wire win over The BANK of Texas makes it three straight — and sits just a game behind the Rats for second place in Combined.

Period 14 Final Scores & Recaps

Goldy Goldy Gone def. Sidd Finch

516.33 – 480.67

Week's High-Water Mark

Goldy needed a big Sunday to steal this one, and got it. Sidd Finch pushed the league's second-highest total of the week and still walked away with a loss, undone by Goldy's late-week surge. Otto Lopez and Kyle Karros both featured among the week's top ten hitters for Goldy, while Eury Perez turned in the league's second-best pitching line. Sidd Finch got a strong week from Nolan McLean and Sandy Alcantara but couldn't cover the gap.

Desert Rats def. Midland Red Weasel Dogs

493.33 – 435.67

Pitching No-Show

The Rats capitalized on what was, charitably, a no-show week from Midland's pitching staff, building an early lead and never letting go. Jake McCarthy and Dylan Cease both cracked the league's top-ten leaderboards for the Rats, who improved to 10-4 and stayed within a game of first place. Midland's offense held up its end — the Weasel Dogs are second in the league in hitting FPts on the season — but the staff couldn't keep pace.

Sons of Pitches def. Houston Gamblers

352.33 – 344.17

Winners of Two Straight

The Sons win again — by 8 this time — but a win is a win, and it's the second in a row for a team that's spent most of the season at the bottom of Combined. Dansby Swanson and Yordan Alvarez both landed on the week's top hitter list. Houston's Ryan Weathers pickup didn't have time to make an impact; the Gamblers fall to 7-7.

Troutdale Cascades def. Ithaca Spartans

421.00 – 361.33

First Place Business

Business as usual for the league's best team. Trey Yesavage delivered another quality pitching week to help the Cascades to 11-3, extending their win streak and their lead atop the Combined standings. Ithaca falls to 4-10 and last place, its pitching staff still last in the league in points by a wide margin — the on-again, off-again jokes about the franchise for sale write themselves at this point.

Troutdale Gales def. Tempe Scorpions

385.67 – 309.00

Playoff Race Statement

Gales put the hurt on the Scorpions in a game that had real playoff-race stakes — both teams entered 6-8 and jockeying for position in a crowded middle tier. Sean Burke's strong week on the mound (fourth among all pitchers) set the tone. Tempe never found an answer, falling to 6-8 with a share of the wild-card picture now a little more crowded behind them.

Newberry Indians def. The BANK of Texas

366.67 – 298.67

Wire to Wire

Solid, start to finish — Newberry never trailed. Yoshinobu Yamamoto's week ranked among the league's best pitching lines, and the Indians' league-leading roster churn (more moves than any other team over Periods 97-104) is clearly paying off. It's three straight wins for Newberry, now 9-5 and firmly in the second-place conversation. The BANK falls to 5-9 and has now lost four in a row.

Free Agency & Waiver Wire — Periods 97 through 104

The wire caught fire around the July 4th trade deadline. Newberry led the league in transaction volume by a wide margin, Desert Rats ran their usual bullpen carousel, and three teams made real Vickrey commitments to upgrade before the deadline bell.

Big-Ticket Bids

Midland Red Weasel Dogs won Dean Kremer (BAL, SP) for $321, dropping Brady Singer — the largest single bid of the window, addressing a rotation that's been outscored by its offense all year.
The BANK of Texas committed $250 for Jac Caglianone (KC, 1B/OF), dropping Jordan Lawlar, then followed up with $200 for reliever Griffin Jax (TB) days later — real spending for a team trying to snap a four-game slide.
Desert Rats paid $222 to secure Kazuma Okamoto (TOR, 1B/3B) right at the July 4th deadline, their most significant addition of the window and a clear power upgrade for the stretch run.

The Newberry Machine

Newberry Indians touched the wire more than any other franchise across this window — a steady stream of speculative SP/RP claims (Chad Patrick, Eduard Bazardo, Brad Lord, Willi Castro, Spencer Steer, Alec Bohm, and more, several churned multiple times) as the front office searched for the right complementary arms. It's the kind of volume that looks scattershot in the moment and looks like depth once the QS/SVH numbers roll in — and Newberry currently sits 4th in the league in pitching points, so the approach is paying off.

Desert Rats' Bullpen Carousel

True to form, the Rats ran a near-constant rotation of $1 relief claims — Kerkering, Thielbar, Alvarado, Yuki Matsui, Paul Blackburn, Luke Weaver, Jeff Hoffman, Yennier Cano, Bryan King, Mason Barnett — cycling arms in and out to chase favorable matchups and save/hold opportunities rather than committing significant FAAB. Combined with the Okamoto splash, it's a team building offensive firepower while streaming its way through the pitching staff.

From Our Columnists

The Ithaca Question

Joe Falls

There comes a point in every fantasy season where a franchise stops being a team and starts being a cautionary tale. Ithaca Spartans crossed that line sometime around Period 90, and Week 14's loss to Troutdale — their tenth of the year against just four wins — didn't so much confirm it as notarize it. A pitching staff that ranks dead last in the league by nearly 250 points doesn't get fixed with a waiver claim here or there. It gets fixed with an offseason and a hard look in the mirror. Whether ownership actually entertains selling the franchise or that's just clubhouse gallows humor, the truth is the same either way: next year starts now for Ithaca, and it should start on the mound.

Three Straight, and Counting

Mitch Albom

Nobody circles Newberry Indians on the schedule in April. Nine and five doesn't scream contender, not with Troutdale sitting at eleven wins and Desert Rats breathing down its neck. But watch the process, not just the record: a pitching staff built one waiver claim at a time, now fourth in the league in points. A win over The BANK that was, in the recap's own words, solid from start to finish — no fireworks required. Three in a row is a streak. What Newberry is building looks more like a system. The Road Ahead is about as gentle as it gets for a contender — Ithaca, Tempe, Midland, Sidd, Sons — and if the Indians don't turn that into five more wins, the conversation changes fast.

Big Lou's Wire Report: Who's Actually Buying?

Big Lou Palmieri

Deadline week separates the men from the boys, and I got receipts. Midland drops three hundred and change on Dean Kremer — that's not a hedge, that's a statement. Desert Rats go get Okamoto for two-twenty-two right as the buzzer sounds. The BANK throws real money at Caglianone and then Griffin Jax days later, which tells me somebody in that front office knows the record doesn't reflect the roster. Meanwhile Newberry's out here collecting relievers like bottle caps, one-dollar bids left and right, and I gotta be honest — the results say don't knock it. Everybody's got a way of building a bullpen. Not everybody's way is working. Newberry's is.
