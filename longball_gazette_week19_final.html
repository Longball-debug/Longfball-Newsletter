Week 19 Final — August 10–16, 2026  ·  Regular Season Complete
NINETEEN WEEKS DOWN. CASCADES FINISH ON TOP. THE FIELD OF TWELVE IS SET.
The Troutdale Cascades closed the regular season the way they played all of it — overwhelmingly, 566 to 279.83 over Houston Gamblers, their sixth straight win and a final record of 16-3. Behind them, a Desert Rats team riding its own three-game streak locked up the No. 2 seed, and the final two contested playoff spots both broke on tiebreakers. Eighteen weeks of grinding are over. Two six-team brackets — one for the trophy, one for next year's No. 1 pick — begin this week.
By the Longball Gazette Wire Staff
League Headquarters — Sunday, August 16, 2026

The Troutdale Cascades were never really in doubt. A 16-3 finish, a 6-game win streak carrying them into the postseason, and a 566-point explosion in the finale against a Houston Gamblers team that finishes 7-12 and misses the money bracket entirely. Cascades earn the No. 1 overall seed and a bye through Round 1. The Desert Rats, the league's own commissioner-managed club, weren't far behind — a 408.33-to-306.17 win over The BANK of Texas closes out a 14-5 season and locks the No. 2 seed, also with a bye. Cascades and Rats have separated themselves from the pack all year, and both get to watch Round 1 from the sideline.

The Newberry Indians claimed the Red Division outright with a 418–328.67 win over the Sons of Pitches, finishing 13-6 and grabbing the No. 3 seed. That sets up a Round 1 date with whichever of Tempe Scorpions or... no, correction — Newberry actually opens Round 1 itself, drawing the Scorpions in the 3-vs-6 matchup, while Goldy Goldy Gone (12-7, No. 4 seed) draws the Midland Red Weasel Dogs (10-9, No. 5 seed) in the other Round 1 game. Full seeding and matchups are broken down on the Playoff Brackets page.

The two closest races in the league both went final on Sunday. In the Wild Card chase, the Midland Red Weasel Dogs (10-9) held off the Troutdale Gales (9-10) for the final money-bracket spot on a head-to-head tiebreaker — and made sure of it by beating the Tempe Scorpions 353.5–225.83 in the finale while the Gales fell to Goldy Goldy Gone, 376.17–367.17, in a game that came down to nine points. In the White Division, Tempe Scorpions and Houston Gamblers finished dead even at 7-12 apiece; Scorpions took the division's automatic playoff bid on the tiebreaker, sending the Gamblers — losers of five straight — into the draft bracket instead.

The Other Bracket: Six Teams Playing For Next Year

While six teams chase the 2026 trophy, the other six are playing an equally real game: the draft bracket, where losing early means picking early, and the team that wins the whole thing claims the No. 1 overall pick in next year's draft. Troutdale Gales (9-10) and Sidd Finch (8-11) enter as the top two seeds and draw byes into Round 2. The BANK of Texas, Houston Gamblers, Sons of Pitches, and Ithaca Spartans open Round 1 this week. The Ithaca Spartans arrive on an 8-game losing streak and a league-worst 4-15 record — but in this bracket, that just means they have the most ground to make up, not the least incentive to try.

Detmers and Burleson Cap Sidd Finch's Best Week of the Second Half

Sidd Finch didn't make the money bracket, but they went out playing like a team that belonged. Reid Detmers turned in the week's single best pitching line at 62 fantasy points, and Alec Burleson led all hitters with 52, powering a 398.67–228.17 rout of Ithaca Spartans. That result is exactly why Sidd Finch enters the draft bracket as the No. 2 seed with a bye — the finale was as much a statement as it was a formality.

Money Bracket: Who Has the Edge?

Troutdale Cascades enter as the clear favorite — a 16-3 record, a six-game win streak, and a 566-point finale that suggests a team playing its best baseball at exactly the right time. Desert Rats are the only club that finished within shouting distance of them all year, and a rested squad with a bye is never one to dismiss. The most interesting Round 1 game is Goldy Goldy Gone (1-game win streak, survived a 9-point scare) against a Midland Red Weasel Dogs team that just won by 128 points to punch its ticket. Newberry Indians, fresh off a division title, draw a Tempe Scorpions team that back-doored into the bracket on a tiebreaker — a dangerous combination of house money and nothing to lose.

Draft Bracket: Who Wants It Least?

This is the strangest kind of competition in fantasy sports — six teams that all missed the playoffs, half of them trying to win their way to the top of next year's draft, the other half perhaps quietly content to lose their way there instead. Ithaca Spartans, losers of eight straight, open against a BANK of Texas team with nearly double their win total. Houston Gamblers and Sons of Pitches is the true coin-flip of Round 1. And with byes in hand, Troutdale Gales and Sidd Finch — both of whom just posted their best week of the stretch run — sit and watch, waiting to see who they draw in Round 2.

Duke Falcone. Marty Aldrich. Big Lou Palmieri. The regular season is closed. Here is who they like heading into the brackets.
Six Games, Two Tiebreakers, No Excuses
By Duke Falcone  ·  The Longball Gazette

Nineteen weeks. That's how long it took to sort twelve teams into two brackets, and in the end it came down to tiebreakers in two of the six playoff spots. That's not a flaw in the system. That's the system working exactly as designed. You don't get style points in this league. You get a seed number, and this week two teams got the good kind and two teams got sent to play for draft position instead.

Troutdale Cascades finish 16-3 with a six-game win streak and a 566-point exclamation point on the way out. I don't need three more paragraphs to tell you they're the favorite. Sixteen and three tells you everything. The only question left is whether anybody in this bracket has the ceiling to survive them for three straight weeks, and right now I don't see it.

Here's what I like about the Midland Red Weasel Dogs sneaking in as the No. 5 seed: they won the game that mattered by 128 points. That's not a team squeaking in. That's a team that showed up when the standings were on the line and made the Gales' decision for them. Don't sleep on a five-seed that just did that against a division opponent with a bye on the line.

And to the Desert Rats: yes, you're the No. 2 seed, yes, you're on a three-game streak, and yes, I know exactly whose newsletter this is. Doesn't change the analysis. You earned the bye. Now go prove it means something.

What the Draft Bracket Is Really About
By Marty Aldrich  ·  The Longball Gazette

There is a particular kind of grace required to play meaningful baseball when the trophy is already out of reach. Six teams enter the draft bracket this week, and the easy read is that they are playing for nothing — that the season already told its story and this is just the epilogue. I don't think that's right. I think the draft bracket is the most honest competition in this league, because every team in it has already lost the thing it wanted most, and what's left is a choice about how to finish anyway.

Ithaca Spartans lost eight straight games to close a 4-15 season, and then turned around and spent $300 in FAAB on Jackson Jobe on their way out the door. I keep coming back to that decision. A team with nothing forced left to prove, still investing like next week matters. There is something worth respecting in that, whatever the standings say.

Troutdale Gales lost their finale by nine points — the closest game of the week — and that nine-point margin is the entire difference between playing meaningful baseball for a trophy and playing meaningful baseball for a draft pick. Nine points. I don't think the Gales have stopped thinking about that game, and I don't think they should. It's the kind of loss that either haunts a franchise or fuels one, and we'll find out which over the next three weeks.

Sidd Finch closed with the two best individual performances in the entire league — Detmers and Burleson, both leading their respective boards — in a game that had zero bearing on their playoff positioning beyond seeding within a bracket that isn't the one with the trophy. They didn't have to play like that. They did anyway. That tells you something about who's managing that roster.

Big Lou’s Corner: The Rats Are the Favorite and I Won’t Apologize For Saying It
By Lou Palmieri, Baseball Lifer  ·  The Longball Gazette

Everybody wants to talk about the Cascades. Sixteen and three, six straight, five hundred and sixty-six points in the finale, fine, I see it, I'm not blind. But let's talk about who's sitting right behind them with a bye of their own: the DESERT RATS. Fourteen and five. Three straight wins. And run by a commissioner who I am contractually obligated to needle every single week, so let's not stop now — how do you go 14-5 and still spend the whole season getting outkicked in the headline race? Second place all year. SECOND. In a league you run! There's a joke in there somewhere about grading your own homework and it still coming back a B-plus.

Now, credit where it's due, because Big Lou calls it straight: the Rats have been the second-best team in this league from wire to wire and they're getting the bye they've earned. If Cascades so much as sneeze in Round 2, the Rats are sitting right there. I've got Cascades over Rats in the final if it comes to that, but I wouldn't bet the house on it, and I definitely wouldn't bet it in front of the commissioner.

TEMPE SCORPIONS. You lost your finale by 128 POINTS and you're still in the bracket because your division was bad enough to let you in on a tiebreaker over a team that finished THE EXACT SAME RECORD.
