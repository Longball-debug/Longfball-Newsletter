Week 16 Final — July 20–26, 2026
SONS SHOCK THE COMMISH; GOLDY GOES OFF; CASCADES SURVIVE A SCARE
The Desert Rats came out of the All-Star break looking like a team that forgot how the game is played, and the Sons of Pitches made them pay for it. Goldy Goldy Gone posted the week's biggest number by a mile. The BANK survived last-place Ithaca by the length of a bunt single. And in Troutdale, the league's best team needed every last point to hold off the Weasel Dogs. Three scoring periods remain. The playoff race just got a lot more interesting.
By the Longball Gazette Wire Staff
League Headquarters — Sunday, July 26, 2026

The Desert Rats went into the All-Star break at 11-4 and looking every bit like a team built to chase the Cascades down the stretch. They came out of it 11-5, blindsided by a Sons of Pitches team that had lost four of five entering the week. Sons of Pitches 558, Desert Rats 528 — a thirty-point margin that will not show up as a blowout on paper but landed like one in the standings, because the commissioner's own team now has company at 11-5 rather than daylight over the field. Whatever went wrong for the Rats coming out of the break, it needs fixing fast. There are only three scoring periods left.

Nobody in this league scored more baseball than Goldy Goldy Gone this week. Goldy dropped 682.67 points on the Houston Gamblers — the week's highest total by a comfortable margin — in a 682.67 to 535.83 rout that pushed Goldy to 10-6 and kept their three-game winning streak alive. The Gamblers, meanwhile, are the most fascinating case in the league right now: 7-9 overall, a losing record by any measure, and yet sitting atop the White Division by a single game. Tuesday's beatdown didn't cost them that lead. It might cost them their margin for error.

The closest game of the week belonged to The BANK of Texas, who needed until the final tally to put away the last-place Ithaca Spartans, 538.83 to 525.33. A 13.5-point escape act keeps the BANK's two-game winning streak intact and puts them one game back of the final wildcard spot. Newberry Indians, meanwhile, keep doing what they've done all season — win without fanfare. Their 549.33 to 492.5 decision over Tempe Scorpions is the Indians' fifth consecutive victory, and it's getting harder to find anyone in this league still overlooking them.

Cascades Survive a Genuine Scare

Troutdale Cascades 675.33, Midland Red Weasel Dogs 659.83. Fifteen and a half points. That's the entire margin between the best record in MLBLONGBALL and a Weasel Dogs team that came within a single big inning of pulling off the upset of the season. The Cascades move to 13-3 and still sit atop the Blue Division and the overall standings by two full games, but for one week the league's model of consistency needed every last point they could scrounge up. The Weasel Dogs, at 8-8, made a costly mistake somewhere in roster management this week — and it cost them a signature win that would have been the best of their season.

Gales Crunch Sidd Finch; Sidd's Skid Reaches Five

The Troutdale Gales handled Sidd Finch decisively, 617.17 to 482.83, extending Sidd's losing streak to five straight games and pushing their record to 6-10. The Gales improve to 7-9 and snap back into the wildcard conversation, one game behind the BANK for the sixth and final playoff spot. Sidd Finch's season, once defined by boom weeks that could outscore the entire league, is now defined by a five-game skid with three scoring periods left to turn it around.

The Break Doesn't Care Who You Are
By Joe Falls, J.G. Taylor Spink Award Winner  ·  The Longball Gazette

Here is what the All-Star break does: it stops everything for a few days, and then it starts again, and some teams remember how to play and some teams don't. The Desert Rats didn't. Sons of Pitches 558, Desert Rats 528. Write it down plainly, because that's how it happened. A team carrying a losing record beat a team that had been building toward the top of this league all season, and there is no complicated explanation for it. The Rats came out flat. It happens to good teams. It happened to this one at the worst possible time, with Newberry Indians waiting to pounce on the tie in the standings.

Goldy Goldy Gone posted 682.67 points against Houston this week. I have covered a lot of lopsided weeks in this league and that number still made me sit up. That's not a team having a good week. That's a team reminding everybody what a peaking roster looks like three periods before the finish line.

The BANK beat Ithaca by 13.5 points and I want to be clear about what that means: not much, and everything. Not much, because Ithaca is 4-12 and nine games out. Everything, because The BANK needed it, got it, and is now one game from the playoff line instead of two. In a league this tight down the stretch, ugly wins count exactly the same as pretty ones.

Houston Gamblers lead the White Division at 7-9. Five Wins Nobody's Talking About
By Mitch Albom  ·  Detroit Free Press  ·  The Longball Gazette

There is a particular kind of losing streak that becomes its own gravity. Not one bad week, but five, each one pulling a little harder than the last, until a team that once looked capable of anything starts to look like it's waiting for the season to end rather than trying to change it. That is where Sidd Finch sits tonight. Five straight losses. A cumulative points total that is still, on paper, better than four teams ranked ahead of them. The math says this roster can still play. The record says otherwise. Somewhere between those two truths is the actual story, and it is not a comfortable one to write.

Contrast that with the Newberry Indians, who are doing the opposite thing in complete silence. Five straight wins. Nobody is writing about it, because there's no dramatic collapse to describe, no signature blowout to marvel at — just a team beating the team in front of them, week after week, with the kind of quiet professionalism that never makes a headline until the standings force you to notice it. They are tied with the Desert Rats at 11-5 now. The gap between "hot streak" and "who this team actually is" has closed completely.

I want to talk about the Weasel Dogs for a moment, because losing by 15.5 points to the best team in the league is not a footnote — it's a data point. CJ Abrams, Michael Harris II, and Jackson Merrill are all producing at a top-ten level right now. That's not luck. That's a roster with real firepower that came within one big swing of the best win of its season. The record says 8-8. The performance says something better than that is coming.

And then there is Houston, sitting atop the White Division with a losing record, which is either the most fragile playoff position in the league or the most efficient one, depending on how you want to tell the story. I don't think Houston cares which version is true. A division title gets you into October regardless of how you got there. The Gamblers just need to survive three more weeks of a division that no longer wants to let them have it easily.
