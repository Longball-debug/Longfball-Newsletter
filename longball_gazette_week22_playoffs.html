Draft Pick Playoff Final  ·  Championship, Week 1 of 2
THE BANK OF TEXAS CLAIMS NO. 1; CASCADES STUN THE RATS TO SEIZE THE TITLE LEAD
Two playoff brackets, two very different Sundays. The Bank of Texas closed out the draft pick playoff in style, while the League Championship flipped entirely in the final 48 hours — and Fantrax likes Troutdale big heading into the deciding week.
By the Longball Gazette Wire Staff
League Headquarters — Sunday, September 6, 2026

The Bank of Texas is your 2026 offseason winner, securing the first pick in next year's draft with a 453–390 victory over the Troutdale Gales. The matchup swung with the weekend — Texas built a 338–275 lead by Friday behind a 33.5-point outburst from Jared Jones, only for the Gales to answer with a 44.67–37.0 Saturday and close to within 55. But The Bank held the line Sunday, closing it out on the strength of a staff that simply prevented more damage: 24 earned runs allowed against Troutdale's 39, despite giving up 10 fewer innings on the mound.

Ronald Acúña Jr. was the offensive engine all week, going 8-for-20 with 3 home runs and 8 RBI for a matchup-high 66.39 fantasy points. Jared Jones (39.37 FP) anchored the rotation with a 6 IP, 0 ER, 9-strikeout gem, and Josh Hader slammed the door with 2 saves/holds down the stretch. Troutdale got everything it could have asked for from Elly De La Cruz (56.58 FP) and a two-start, 15-strikeout week from Taj Bradley — it simply wasn't enough. The Bank of Texas will pick first in 2026.

Cascades Erase a 94-Point Deficit to Seize the Title Lead

If the draft playoff was a coronation, the League Championship was a demolition derby. The Desert Rats controlled Week 1 of the two-week final almost wire to wire — building a commanding 306–212 lead by Friday behind Alex Bregman's three-homer Monday and a 28.67-point pitching day from Logan Henderson and Jake Bennett on Thursday. Then Troutdale's offense woke up. Over the final two days the Cascades erupted for 15 home runs and 43 RBI on the week to the Rats' 6 and 23, and a monstrous two-start week from Bryan Woo (14 IP, 1 ER, 16 K, 84.42 FP — the single best individual performance of either playoff bracket) powered a Troutdale pitching staff that out-worked Mesa's, 80.1 innings to 66.2. The final: Cascades 414.17, Desert Rats 386.33 (890.80–813.94 in total raw fantasy points) — a stunning turnaround that wiped out a 94-point deficit in 48 hours.

Jake McCarthy (71.38 FP, 4 SB) was Troutdale's offensive spark plug, while Alex Bregman (64.10 FP, 4 HR, 8 RBI) and a clean Blake Snell quality start (49.21 FP) kept the Rats afloat before the weekend collapse. With one week to go, the Desert Rats trail for the first time in the series — and now have to make up ground without a fully healthy Shohei Ohtani, whose 55.91-point Week 1 (three runs, two walks, two hit-by-pitches, a stolen base) came almost entirely without hits. If that production is limited or gone for the final week, Bregman, Corey Seager, and the rest of the lineup will need to cover the gap alone.
