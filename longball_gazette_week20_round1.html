Playoff Round 1 Recap — MLBLONGBALL Postseason
SCORPIONS STING NO. 3 SEED; SONS RIDE TATIS; BANK SURVIVES A DOGFIGHT
The MLBLONGBALL postseason opened with a bracket-shaking upset, a Fernando Tatis Jr. masterpiece, and a two-touchdown escape act. Four teams punch their tickets to Round 2. Four teams go home. And the four bye-week teams — Troutdale Cascades, Desert Rats, Troutdale Gales, and Sidd Finch — watched it all from the couch.
By the Longball Gazette Wire Staff
League Headquarters — Sunday, August 23, 2026

If you had circled the Tempe Scorpions as a sleeper entering the postseason, you have every right to feel smug this morning. The 6-seed Scorpions did not just beat the 3-seed Newberry Indians — they buried them, 492.17 to 363.33, the largest margin of victory in Round 1 by a wide berth. A .303 team batting average, 17 home runs, and a pitching staff that struck out 64 while posting a 3.20 ERA turned what many expected to be a competitive first-round game into a rout. Newberry, by contrast, hit .222 as a team and finished on the wrong side of a 4-7 pitching record for the week. The Scorpions advance to face the No. 2 seed Desert Rats in Round 2. Newberry's season is over.

Elsewhere in the Championship Bracket, Goldy Goldy Gone did what Goldy Goldy Gone does — they overwhelmed the waiver wire and then went out and won the game anyway. Forty-six separate roster transactions over the course of the week (a league-high, and it isn't particularly close) preceded a 397.33-to-352.67 win over the Midland Red Weasel Dogs. Goldy advances to face the top-seeded Troutdale Cascades, fresh off their bye, in Round 2.

In the Draft Bracket, Fernando Tatis Jr. put on a show. The Sons of Pitches slugger went off for a 59-point game — 12-for-25 with six runs, four home runs, and 11 RBI — to power the 10-seed Sons past the Houston Gamblers, 332.67 to 282.83. The Sons hit .318 as a team, the best cumulative average of any club in Round 1. They move on to face top-seeded Troutdale Gales in Round 2. The BANK of Texas, meanwhile, survived the round's closest call, edging the Ithaca Spartans 264.00 to 242.67 — a 21-point margin built despite a bullpen ERA (6.18) that would make most Championship Bracket teams blush.

Round 2 Is Set

The bracket now sends the No. 1 seed Troutdale Cascades against a hot Goldy Goldy Gone club, and the No. 2 seed Desert Rats — anchored by Dylan Cease's 21.36 FP/G — against the red-hot Tempe Scorpions coming off their upset. In the Draft Bracket, Troutdale Gales get the Tatis-fueled Sons of Pitches, while Sidd Finch, rested off a bye, draws The BANK of Texas. Full previews inside.

The largest margin of Round 1, and it belonged to the lowest seed to win a game. The Scorpions hit .303 as a team (79-for-261) with 17 home runs, 51 RBI, and 49 runs scored — an offensive week that would have been the best of almost any regular-season round this year. The pitching staff matched it: 7-2 with a 3.20 ERA and 64 strikeouts across 56.1 innings. Newberry, the No. 3 seed, simply had no answer. The Indians hit .222 as a team and their pitching staff went 4-7 despite throwing nearly 30 more innings (85.2) than the Scorpions.

This is the kind of week that makes a No. 6 seed dangerous heading into a matchup with the No. 2 seed Desert Rats. Newberry's season ends in the first round after a Red Division-leading regular season — a reminder that in a one-week playoff format, seeding buys you a bye, not a guarantee.

Goldy Goldy Gone did not so much win this game as out-manage it. Forty-six transactions over the course of the week — nearly every waiver-wire arm in the league cycled through their roster at some point — produced a pitching staff that logged 88.2 innings with 86 strikeouts, the most volume of any team in Round 1. The offense chipped in a modest .220 average but drew 46 walks, the most in the round, and that patience turned into 42 runs scored. Midland countered with a respectable .237 average and a tidy 3.44 ERA, but 73.1 innings of pitching simply couldn't match Goldy's workload.

The volume strategy gets its biggest test yet in Round 2: Goldy draws the top-seeded, well-rested Troutdale Cascades.

Fernando Tatis Jr. carried this game on his back. A 12-for-25 week with six runs, four home runs, and 11 RBI netted 59.0 fantasy points — the single best individual hitting performance of Round 1 — and it was the difference-maker in a Sons of Pitches offense that hit .318 as a team, tops among all eight clubs that played. Houston hit .261 with 8 home runs and 7 stolen bases, a perfectly respectable week, but their pitching staff went just 1-8 with a 4.52 ERA and that was too much to overcome.

The Sons move on to face the top-seeded Troutdale Gales in Round 2, riding the hottest bat in the league.

The tightest game of the round, and not a pretty one for either bullpen. The BANK of Texas won despite a pitching staff that allowed 46 earned runs in 67 innings — a 6.18 ERA and 13 home runs surrendered, both the worst marks among Round 1 winners. What saved them was thump: 9 team home runs and 38 RBI on a modest .228 average. Ithaca countered with a low-power, low-damage week — just 3 home runs and 16 RBI — and their own pitching staff wasn't much better, coughing up 17 home runs, the most allowed by any team in Round 1.

Two shaky pitching staffs, one 21-point margin. The BANK survives to face a well-rested Sidd Finch club in Round 2, and they'll need considerably more from the mound to get past them.

The Longball Gazette Columnists
Six Seed, No Fear
By Duke Falcone  ·  The Longball Gazette

Here's what I know: a No. 3 seed just got run off the field by 128 points, and the team that did it is a six seed nobody circled in June. Tempe Scorpions hit .303 as a team. Seventeen home runs. A 3.20 ERA from the mound. That is not an upset, that is a statement, and the Desert Rats better be watching tape instead of celebrating their bye week.

Newberry Indians won a division. Newberry Indians hit .222 in the one week that decided their season. I'm not going to pretend those two facts don't sit uneasily next to each other. A regular season is a marathon. A one-week playoff game is a sprint, and Newberry got left at the blocks.

Now the Rats draw the hottest team in the bracket. Dylan Cease is sitting on 21.36 FP/G and a 2.37 ERA, fresh off a bye, and that is a legitimate weapon. But the Scorpions just showed they can produce a ceiling week out of nowhere. If Tempe brings anything close to Round 1 into this matchup, the No. 2 seed has a real problem on its hands.

One more thing before I go: Goldy Goldy Gone made 46 transactions to win a game by 44 points. I don't know if that's genius team management or a front office that can't sit still. Ask me again after they play the Cascades.

The Ones Who Waited
By Marty Aldrich  ·  The Longball Gazette

There is a particular kind of quiet that belongs to a team on a bye week during the first round of the playoffs. Four franchises in this league experienced it this week — Troutdale Cascades, Desert Rats, Troutdale Gales, Sidd Finch — and none of them touched a single scoring period while the rest of the bracket fought for its life. It is a strange privilege, watching other people's seasons end while yours simply continues, untouched, into the next round.

Sidd Finch didn't play a game this week, and two of their hitters still cracked the league's individual top five. Pete Crow-Armstrong put up 60 points. Bobby Witt Jr. put up 49.5. Those numbers happened in a vacuum, uncounted, unused, and they will mean nothing in the standings — but they mean something to The BANK of Texas, who now has to face a roster capable of producing that kind of week on command.

I keep coming back to Newberry Indians. A Red Division champion, eliminated in the first round, by a team seeded three spots below them. There is no story here about a team that didn't deserve its season — the record was earned over five months. There is only the fact that fantasy baseball, like the real thing, does not care what you did in April. It asks what you can do this week, and this week the Scorpions had an answer Newberry didn't.

The BANK of Texas survived their game by 21 points while allowing a 6.18 ERA. I don't know what to make of a team that wins while its pitching staff is actively on fire. Perhaps that is simply what survival looks like sometimes — not clean, not convincing, just enough.

Big Lou’s Corner: Forty-Six Moves and Nobody Got Hurt
By Lou Palmieri, Baseball Lifer  ·  The Longball Gazette

FORTY-SIX TRANSACTIONS. I counted it twice because I did not believe the first count. Goldy Goldy Gone ran through more waiver moves in ONE WEEK than some teams in this league have made all SEASON, and then they went out and WON THEIR PLAYOFF GAME. Hao-Yu Lee got added, dropped, and added again IN THE SAME DAY. I don't know if that's a fantasy team or a Wall Street trading desk, but whatever it is, it's 1-0 in the postseason and playing the Cascades next. Somebody buy that GM a coffee. Or take the coffee away. I genuinely cannot tell which one helps.

TEMPE SCORPIONS made SIX transactions all week and posted FOUR HUNDRED NINETY-TWO POINTS. Six! Goldy made forty-six and put up 397! I am begging somebody in this league to explain the math to me because right now it looks like the answer to "how many moves should I make" is either "all of them" or "almost none," and there is no in-between that works.

Let's talk about the Commish's own Desert Rats for a second because I love a good self-own. TWO HUNDRED TWENTY-SIX DOLLARS on Jared Young. Won the bid. Two days later? DROPPED HIM for Mauricio Dubon. Two hundred twenty-six dollars of FAAB, gone, for a player who didn't survive the week. I don't make the rules of this newsletter but I do get to point out when the man who does make the rules loses a bidding war to his own bench. That's not an insult, Commish, that's just accounting.

And THE BANK OF TEXAS. Six-point-one-eight ERA on the mound. THIRTEEN home runs allowed. And they WIN by 21 points anyway. That is not pitching, that is hostage negotiation. They survive to play Sidd Finch next round, and Sidd Finch has two guys who just put up top-5 individual weeks without even suiting up. I would not want to be the BANK's bullpen coach right now. I would not want to be anywhere near that bullpen right now.
