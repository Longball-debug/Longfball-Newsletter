Week 15 Final — Scoring Period 15, 2026
CASCADES AND RATS WON'T BLINK; DOGS EXPLODE FOR 454; BANK SURVIVES BY 1.17
The top of MLBLONGBALL stayed exactly where it was — Troutdale Cascades and Desert Rats both won again, and the gap is still one game. Newberry Indians made it four wins in a row and were just as busy off the field, leading the league with 23 separate waiver claims. And in the tightest finish since the two-point classic of Week 12, The BANK of Texas held off Houston Gamblers by a single point one seven.
By the Longball Gazette Wire Staff
League Headquarters — Sunday, July 12, 2026

The race at the top of MLBLONGBALL refuses to change shape. The Troutdale Cascades handled the Tempe Scorpions with ease, 442.50 to 270.67, and stay at 12-3 — still the best record and the best cumulative point total in the league. The Desert Rats, one game behind entering the week, took care of business too, edging Sidd Finch 394.67 to 387.33 to hold at 11-4. Same as it's been for weeks: Cascades on top, Rats one game back, and nobody flinching.

The Newberry Indians are the league's hottest team, full stop. A 409.50-to-279.17 dismantling of the Ithaca Spartans pushed Newberry to 10-5 and extended their winning streak to four games. They didn't slow down off the field either: Newberry submitted 23 separate waiver claims this week — more activity than the next two most active teams combined — as Taj Bradley (54.5 pts) and Brandon Pfaadt (33.67 pts) both delivered top-10 pitching weeks for a roster that is clearly not sitting still.

And then there was the closest game of the week, maybe the season: The BANK of Texas needed every bit of a 351.50-to-350.33 win over the Houston Gamblers to improve to 6-9. The margin, 1.17 points, is tighter than the two-point classic between the Sons of Pitches and Sidd Finch that this paper covered back in Week 12. Ben Rice (55.5 pts) and Juan Soto (41.5 pts) both cracked the hitting top-10 for the BANK. Brice Turang's 47-point week for Houston wasn't quite enough to cover the gap.

Dogs Put Up 454; James Wood Has the Week's Best Line in the League

The Midland Red Weasel Dogs posted the week's second-highest score, 454 to 385 over the Troutdale Gales, and moved to 8-7. James Wood led all hitters in MLBLONGBALL with 67 fantasy points (10-for-20, 5 HR), and the Dogs got a full team effort behind him — Cam Schlittler (49.33 pts) and Paul Skenes (39.67 pts) both landed in the pitching top-10. Three Dogs in two different top-10 leaderboards in the same week is a rare thing.

Wheeler Throws the Best Game of the Season — and Loses Anyway

Zack Wheeler's line for the Sons of Pitches this week is the best pitching performance MLBLONGBALL has produced all year: 74 fantasy points on 13 innings, a 0.69 ERA, and 24 strikeouts. The Sons lost anyway, falling 401 to 440.17 against Goldy Goldy Gone, and drop to 5-10. It is the second time this season this paper has had to write a sentence like that — individual brilliance, team result unchanged.

Week 15 — Free Agent & Vickrey Auction Activity

158 transactions across the league from July 5–12. Newberry Indians ran the table with 23 separate claims. And as the league pushes toward Week 19 — the final week of the 2026 season — the FAAB bids tell their own story: this is a league spending like there's no next year, because for this roster, there isn't.
💰 THE BIDS ARE GETTING BIGGER AS THE FINISH LINE APPROACHES

Early in the week, this was a league of one-dollar minimum claims and modest $15–$25 bids — Newberry Indians alone picked up Zebby Matthews, Ryan Zeferjahn, Gregory Soto, and Walbert Urena for $15–$23 apiece. By the back half of the week, four different teams cut FAAB checks of $300 or more, and two teams broke the $450 mark entirely. Opinion & Commentary — Week 15 Final
The Columnists
Joe Falls. Mitch Albom. Big Lou Palmieri. Wheeler threw the game of the year and lost. The BANK won by a point-one-seven. And the league is spending like Week 19 is already here — because it basically is.
One Game. Still One Game. That's the Whole Story at the Top.
By Joe Falls  ·  J.G. Taylor Spink Award Winner, 2001  ·  The Longball Gazette

I keep waiting for somebody to blink at the top of this league, and nobody will. The Troutdale Cascades beat Tempe Scorpions by 171.8 points this week. The Desert Rats beat Sidd Finch by 7.3. Different margins, same result: the gap between first and second place is one game, exactly what it was last week, and the week before that. This is not a coincidence anymore. This is two teams that are simply better than everyone else, playing out a season-long staring contest.

The Cascades are 12-3. They lead the league in cumulative points by well over 300 over the Rats, and their point differential — plus-1,340 — isn't close to anyone else's. Payton Tolle threw 9.2 innings at a 0.93 ERA this week and it barely made the highlight reel, because that's what a 442-point week does to a good outing. This is what a dominant team looks like from the top down.

The Rats aren't just hanging around, either. Shohei Ohtani hit for 39.5 points, Dylan Cease posted a shutout line, and the Rats did what they had to do against a Sidd Finch team that made them work for every one of those 7.3 points. Eleven and four with two straight wins. If the Cascades ever do slip, the Rats will be right there. They have been for weeks.

I want to talk about Newberry Indians, because a 10-5 team on a four-game winning streak deserves more attention than it's getting with two juggernauts above it. Newberry beat Ithaca by 130.3 points this week behind Taj Bradley (54.5 pts) and Brandon Pfaadt (33.67 pts) — two starters in the league top 10 on the same roster. And off the field, this team submitted 23 separate waiver claims in a single week. Twenty-three. Nobody else in this league is close to that level of activity. That's not a front office sitting still while a hot streak plays out. That's a front office that thinks the hot streak has room to keep going.

And then there's the matter of the closest game of the week, which wasn't at the top of the standings at all. The BANK of Texas beat Houston Gamblers by 1.17 points. One point one seven. Brice Turang put up 47 for the Gamblers and it still came up short. Ben Rice and Juan Soto both had top-10 weeks for the BANK, and this time, unlike a few weeks back, the production actually converted into a win. Small margins decide fantasy seasons. This league just got another reminder.

Seventy-Four Points and a Loss
By Mitch Albom  ·  Detroit Free Press  ·  The Longball Gazette

There is a number I cannot stop looking at from Week 15: 74.00. That is what Zack Wheeler gave the Sons of Pitches this week — thirteen innings, a 0.69 ERA, twenty-four strikeouts. It is, by a wide margin, the best pitching performance any team in this league has received all season. And the Sons of Pitches lost. 401 to 440.17. I have written a version of this sentence before, back in Week 12, about a different player on a different roster. I did not expect to be writing it again so soon.

What do you do with a game like that? Wheeler did not fail. Wheeler did everything a pitcher can do. The rest of his roster simply could not find enough points around him, and Goldy Goldy Gone — a team that does not make headlines, that just quietly wins — had enough balance to cover a historic outing from the other dugout. Esmerlyn Valdez and Matthew Boyd were not spectacular. They were sufficient. Sometimes sufficient beats spectacular. That is a hard thing to sit with if you're the Sons of Pitches, at 5-10, watching your ace deliver a career week that will not show up anywhere in the standings.

I want to say something about the Tempe Scorpions, because I do not think a 171.8-point loss should be allowed to erase what Ryan O'Hearn and Jordan Walker did this week. Two hitters, both in the top five of the entire league's batting leaderboard. That is not a fluke. That is real production from a real roster. And it happened in a week where the team lost by more than any other game on the board. There is a version of this season where the Scorpions' hitting catches up with their record. There is also a version where it never does, and this becomes the story of a talented team that could never get out of its own way. Six weeks remain to find out which version this is.

And I keep returning to the number 1.17. That is the margin by which The BANK of Texas beat Houston Gamblers this week — the closest game of Scoring Period 15, closer even than the two-point classic this paper covered back in June. Brice Turang did everything he could. It was not, by the smallest possible fraction, enough. A fantasy season is built from moments like this one, decided by little more than a rounding error, and nobody involved will ever fully explain why it went the way it did.

Big Lou's Corner: The League Is Spending Like the Rent's Due
By Lou Palmieri, Baseball Lifer  ·  The Longball Gazette

FIVE HUNDRED DOLLARS. That's what Sidd Finch bid on Will Warren this week. FIVE HUNDRED DOLLARS, same as what The BANK of Texas bid on Nick Gonzales four days earlier. I have watched this league run its Vickrey auction for years and I have never seen bidding get this reckless this late in a season. And you know what? Good. GOOD. Week 19 is the last week of this thing. There is no next season to save FAAB for. If you're sitting on $400 of budget in Week 15 with the finish line four weeks out, you are doing this hobby wrong.

Look at the spread of the week: Alex Bregman for $451 to the Desert Rats. Christian Walker for $402 to the BANK. THREE separate bids over $300 from the Midland Red Weasel Dogs alone — Cedric Mullins, Brayan Rocchio, Geraldo Perdomo. Compare that to Sunday of the same week, when Newberry Indians was picking up middle relievers for FIFTEEN DOLLARS. That's not a typo. Fifteen dollars for a reliever on Monday, five hundred dollars for a hitter by Sunday. That's what a stretch run looks like when everybody finally understands the clock is real.

Now let me talk about who ISN'T spending, because somebody has to. Sons of Pitches: ZERO claims this week. One drop, no pickup, nothing. You are 5-10, your ace just threw a 74-point game and LOST, and you didn't touch the wire? Tempe Scorpions, same story — ZERO claims. You've got two top-five hitters and a pitching staff dragging you to a 171-point loss, and you sat on your hands. I don't understand it. If I'm running either of these rosters, I am on that wire every single day until Week 19.

And can we talk about NEWBERRY INDIANS for a second? Twenty-three claims. TWENTY-THREE. In one week. This team is 10-5, on a FOUR GAME WIN STREAK, and instead of coasting, they're out there churning the bottom of their roster like it's the first week of the season. Chad Patrick, Ryan Zeferjahn, twice each. Walbert Urena. Gregory Soto. This is a front office that does not believe in standing still, and right now it is very hard to argue with the results.
